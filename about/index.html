Purpose 이 사이트를 만든 이유

Because I am a K-pop fan.

If you are one too, you know the stories. The album that only happened because the members kept asking for it. The group that never got another comeback because the last one "didn't sell". I have watched artists I love wait for years for a chance that never came.

Inside the industry, the next album is planned off the last one. Someone looks at the previous first-week number, guesses the next, and decides. One miss makes it very hard to argue for another try.

So I tried to build a forecasting model from public data. I am a fan with some statistics, not an industry insider, and the model is a modest thing: it looks at what an artist has already done and gives a range for the next album.

K-pop is an art about people and feelings. Data does not replace that, and I don't want it to. The data is full of idols and fans who went far past anything a model expected, and those are my favourite cases.

The model gives a deliberately wide range, not a single number, and it is wrong often. Every card says so. Please treat it as a reference and nothing more.

This is a hobby: non-profit, nobody pays for it and nobody is selling anything.

What is 초동? 초동이란

초동 (chodong, "first movement") is the number of copies a K-pop album sells in its first seven days on the Korean charts. Because most K-pop albums are bought by fans in the first week, 초동 is the number the industry uses to size a fandom, to compare comebacks and to decide whether the next album happens. This site forecasts that first-week number before the comeback is announced. You can look up the album-by-album record and the forecast for about 500 groups and soloists.

초동은 앨범 발매 후 첫 7일 동안의 판매량입니다. 케이팝 앨범은 대부분 팬덤이 첫 주에 구매하기 때문에, 초동은 팬덤 규모를 재고 컴백을 비교하고 다음 앨범을 낼지 결정할 때 쓰는 기준 숫자가 되었습니다. 이 사이트는 컴백 발표 전에 그 초동을 예측합니다. 아이돌 그룹과 솔로 가수 500여 팀의 앨범별 초동 기록과 예상 초동을 볼 수 있습니다.

About the clock

The model only uses what is knowable about 120 days before release, which is roughly when a company decides whether to make the album. What it forecasts is the first-week sales figure (초동), the number the industry actually judges a comeback by.

How to read the forecast

Your scenario, not news. The release month, album type and number of versions on the forecast card are what you picked. Change them and the forecast changes. Nothing on this site is news about a real comeback.

Every forecast card shows one number and three purple bands. Sales are counted in copies sold in the first week. Past sales on the timelines show the first two digits and hide the rest on purpose, like 45*,***.

The number is our best guess for the artist's next album. The real number has a 50% chance of coming in above it and a 50% chance of coming in below it.

The three purple bands show how sure we are: a 50% chance the real first week lands inside the darkest band, 80% inside the middle band and 90% inside the widest one. "Likely between" on the card is the middle band, the 80% one. Even the widest band still misses 10% of the time; a band that never missed would be too wide to be useful. Tap the i next to "Likely between" to see the bands with that artist's numbers.

What the model learned from. It learned only from albums released in 2019 or later, because before 2019 we have no record of how fast an artist's Instagram following was growing, one of the signals it uses. Older albums still appear on the timelines and still count as an artist's "previous album".

How do we know? We tested the model the way it is used: for each year from 2022 to 2026, we let it learn only from albums released before that year, then asked it to forecast that year's albums, so it never saw the answers. The 2026 test runs to 3 Sep 2026, where our data ends. That makes 1,140 tested albums, grouped by how many copies the artist's previous album sold in its first week. The bands look longer on the upside because sales move in multiples. Around a guess of 100,000, "twice as much" is 200,000 (+100,000) and "half as much" is 50,000 (−50,000): the same step, but the upward one takes more room on the axis.

When we give no forecast

Sometimes the first-week number we have for an album is too low to be real. We can tell because the official monthly chart for the same album is far higher; normally the two move together. When the first week is below 30% of where it should be, some sales were probably never counted, and our model can't forecast that album. This says the number is incomplete, not that the album did badly.

Such an album gets no reason labels on its timeline, is left out of the accuracy table above, and is never the "last album" a forecast is compared with. If it is the artist's latest album, the forecast card names the earlier album it uses instead.

An artist gets no forecast at all when our database doesn't have enough recent data for them yet.

Data & credits

This site is a hobby research project; it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any chart, retailer, platform or company named below.

The forecasts are produced by a model trained on an internal dataset of physical album sales, compiled from public sales summaries and charts. It is not published here or anywhere else, and it is not shown on this site in raw form. Everything you see — the predicted range, the percentage changes, the tiers, the masked figures such as 14,*** — is derived from it and rounded so that no underlying sales figure can be recovered.

The explanatory signals the model looks at come mainly from these sources, and we are grateful to the people who maintain them:

  • Wikipedia ↗English, Korean and Japanese editions — artist facts, rosters, debut dates, military service and album metadata. Text available under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Korean Sales ↗A fan-maintained blog of per-artist album sales summaries (koreansalestwt.blogspot.com). It is the main public source the internal sales dataset was compiled from, and it also supplies album metadata such as release format and label. No sales figure from it is reproduced on this site.
  • Soridata ↗Freely downloadable Korean music database (CC BY-SA 4.0) — digital-chart aggregates, awards and debut dates.
  • K-Pop Radar ↗Monthly follower counts of official Instagram accounts, used only as growth rates (followers gained per month divided by followers already held). No follower count is shown.
