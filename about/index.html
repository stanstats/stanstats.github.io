Purpose 이 사이트를 만든 이유

Cause I am a K-pop fan.

If you are one too, you know the stories. The album that only happened because the members kept asking for it. The group that never got another comeback because the last one "didn't sell". I have watched artists I love wait for years for a chance that never came.

Inside the industry, the next album is planned off the last one. Someone looks at the previous first-week number, guesses the next, and decides. One miss makes it very hard to argue for another try.

So I tried to build a forecasting model from public data. I am a fan with some statistics, not an industry insider, and the model is a modest thing: it looks at what an artist has already done and gives a range for the next album.

K-pop is an art about people and feelings. Data does not replace that, and I don't want it to. The data is full of idols and fans who went far past anything a model expected, and those are my favourite rows.

The model gives a deliberately wide range, not a single number, and it is wrong often. Every card says so. Please treat it as a reference and nothing more.

This is a hobby: open source, non-profit, nobody pays for it and nobody is selling anything.

About the clock

The model only uses what is knowable about 120 days before release, which is roughly when the "do we make this album?" decision happens. What it forecasts is the first-week sales figure (초동), the number the industry actually judges a comeback by.

How to read the forecast

Every forecast card shows one number and three purple bands.

The number is our middle guess for the artist's next album in first-week copies. Half of the time the real number would be above this, half below.

The three purple bands are how sure we are: there is a 50% chance the real first week lands inside the darkest band, an 80% chance it lands inside the middle band, and a 90% chance it lands inside the widest one. Tap the ⓘ next to "Likely between" on any card to see these bands spelled out with that artist's numbers.

How do we know? We tested the model on past albums, grouped by how big the artist's previous album was. Our guess was typically off by the "typical miss" below either way, and a share of the time it was off by more than double. Those past misses are what set the width of the bands:

The bands are wider on the upside because album sales move in multiples, not in fixed amounts — a jump to the top of the band and a drop to the bottom are the same size in percentage terms.

Data & credits

This site is a hobby research project; it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any chart, retailer, platform or company named below.

The forecasts are produced by a model trained on an internal dataset of physical album sales, compiled from public sales summaries and charts. It is not published here or in the code repository, and it is not shown on this site in raw form. Everything you see — the predicted range, the percentage changes, the tiers, the masked figures such as 14,*** — is derived from it and rounded so that no underlying sales figure can be recovered.

The explanatory signals the model looks at come mainly from these sources, and we are grateful to the people who maintain them:

  • Wikipedia ↗English, Korean and Japanese editions — artist facts, rosters, debut dates, military service and album metadata. Text available under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Korean Sales ↗A fan-maintained blog of per-artist album sales summaries (koreansalestwt.blogspot.com). It is the main public source the internal sales dataset was compiled from, and it also supplies album metadata such as release format and label. No sales figure from it is reproduced on this site.
  • Soridata ↗Freely downloadable Korean music database (CC BY-SA 4.0) — digital-chart aggregates, awards and debut dates. Derived tables we publish keep the same license.
  • K-Pop Radar ↗Monthly follower counts of official Instagram accounts, used only as growth rates (followers gained per month divided by followers already held). No follower count is shown.
  • YouTube ↗Upload dates on official channels, used only for upload cadence (how many videos in recent months). No video, title or view count is shown.
  • Brokerage research reportsYuanta, KB, Samsung and Kiwoom Securities — quoted, with attribution, in the practitioner benchmark in our findings; copyright remains with the publishers.
